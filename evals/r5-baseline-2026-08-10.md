## The item does what it was built to do

Expected accuracy sits at 0.743 rather than R1's ~0.35, and four of seven
questions are answered unanimously. That is the property the slot was created to
get: a regression has somewhere to fall from, and a one-run wobble no longer moves
the headline number.

It also discriminates rather than rewarding a blanket "no" — the one question
whose true answer is *yes* (funding disclosed) scores 5/5, and the content
question (Planck 2018) scores 5/5.

## Two real gaps in what reviews check

`data-availability-statement` and `systematics-separated` are **1/5 each**, and
that is not a scoring artefact — four of five runs contain **zero** occurrences of
"data availability" in any casing.

Both are in `peer-review` Phase 6, which lists a Data row and an Openness-limits
row. They are in the checklist and not reliably executed. The contrast with
`trials-count-quantified` at 5/5 is instructive: the trials question is *salient*
for this paper because the whole dispute is about it, while a missing Data
Availability statement is the kind of routine transparency gap a reviewer skips
when the science is interesting.

That is a finding about the skill, produced on the first run of the item, and it
is exactly the class of thing R1 could not surface.

## Correction to the first score

The first pass reported 0.686 with `systematics-separated` at 0/5. Two patterns
were too narrow, and **both misses were in the scorer, not the runs**:

- run 1 says "Systematic uncertainty is not budgeted separately from statistical" —
  the correct answer, with a verb outside the pattern's list.
- run 5 says "**Pre-registration:** None" — the correct answer, with the negation
  *following* the term rather than preceding it.

Every non-matching run was read before either pattern changed, because widening a
pattern until the number improves is how a key gets fitted to its own runs. The
two genuinely low scores survived that check unchanged.

This is the third scoring-pattern defect found today, after the severity counter's
missing entry format and the verdict extractor's prose forms. The recurring lesson
is not that regexes are brittle — it is that **a low score should be treated as a
claim about the scorer until the runs have been read.**
